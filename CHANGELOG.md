# Changelog

All notable OTRI releases and material methodology changes will be documented here.

## Unreleased

- **A calculator course says which edition it is.** The admin's calculator-courses form has a year field; the year is stored with the course (`races.edition_year`), returned as `edition_year` on the race summary, and shown in the calculator's "Pick a race" list and course header next to the place. Courses change between editions, and a runner should know which one they are looking at.
- **The calculator's course header says climb per kilometre, not the steepest 50 m.** A single extreme said little about a course and the map beneath already shows where the steep ground is and how much there is; "+42 m/km" (or ft per mile) is the figure runners size a course by.
- **The legal pages read as finished.** `/privacy/`, `/terms/` and `/data-policy/` no longer open with a draft notice, a lawyer's caveat or a pointer to the repository file; the data policy's "Legal review" note is gone, and the only line under the title is when the page last changed. Their header carries the site's own mark and name, drawn from the brand geometry like the site's header, instead of a font rendering of the word.
- **Maintenance mode, switched from the admin page.** Admin → Site closes the site with a message in the admin's words; every page asks `GET /site/status` as it opens and shows the notice instead of itself, then asks again every half minute so it opens by itself when the site is back. Admins who are signed in and anyone who enters the site password still see the site; a page that was already open keeps working and shows a banner. The API keeps answering throughout. For the day the API itself is down, the `VITE_OTRI_MAINTENANCE` build variable closes every page whatever the API says (`src/components/Maintenance.jsx`, `docs/operations/digitalocean-deployment.md` §10).
- **The display units reach everywhere, and "How it works" plays its steps.** The example race card on the landing page, the target-time cards and the calculator's "Show the maths" kept kilometres and metres whatever the units menu said; they now follow it like every other page, and the maths block says when its figures are converted. The header and footer logos, pressed on the home page itself, scroll back to the top. The three steps on the home page each have a small scene in the site's round stroke (`src/components/StepArt.jsx`): the files dropping into the tool, the scores appearing under a reading glass, the result going out as a file, a link and a picture; still for anyone who asked their system for less motion.
- **The docs describe the product, not the build.** `docs/roadmap.md` (every phase done, and wrong about what remained) and the pre-model `METHODOLOGY.md` are gone; the research rules from the latter live in `HANDBOOK.md` ("Research discipline"), whose stack, structure, MVP and roadmap sections went with them. `docs/organizer-upload.md` is now `docs/RESULT-FILES.md`, the reader's version of what `ingestion/` recognises. `api/README.md`'s endpoint and environment tables are rebuilt from `api/app.py`; `prototype/README.md` describes the pages that exist; `course/README.md` says `course-measurement-v3` and stops mentioning a scoring version that no longer exists; the build id in the model pages is the one the API returns; OEP-001 and OEP-002 say they were superseded. `docs/README.md` indexes the folder, `README.md`'s tree is the real one, and `.github/` gains a pull request template and three issue templates (something broken, a course that measures wrong, a results file not read right).
- **A password on the door while the site is not yet open.** Every page asks for a password once per browser (`src/components/Gate.jsx`, `GATE_ENABLED`); the legal pages send a visitor without it to the front; `index.html` and `robots.txt` say noindex and disallow for the duration. It is a browser-side check on a static site, not a wall: the API stays public. Opening the site is one constant, one meta line and one robots line.
- **The product lives at the root.** What was `/prototype/` is `otri.run/`, the organizer app is `/organizer/`, the embeddable calculator `/embed/`; the older marketing page is retired, since the landing page is the product. Every link OTRI writes (emails, the API page's iframe snippet, the badge snippet, the redirect pages, the sitemap) says the new address, and the old `/prototype/…` addresses redirect with their hash and query intact, so links already in inboxes and posts keep working. On the landing page the calculator is the first button.
- **The homepage says what you get before it explains the maths.** "Your race. Explained scores. Open for everyone to see." with a genuine preview of the built-in example race scored by the live API (`src/data/example-preview.json`, labelled synthetic): profile, top five, the winner's calculation. Then, in order: share one link and everyone can see the scores; from two files to an explained result in three steps; what your runners get; every score explains itself, with the model's limits; a target time on your course, shown with two real estimates; the five questions people ask before uploading; the example again. The older root page carries the same message and sends visitors into the example instead of to GitHub. "Try an example" is in the header; "A calculator, not a governing body" moved to the FAQ. The product pages are indexable (`noindex` removed from `/prototype/`, `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml` added, titles and descriptions rewritten). The "Scored with OTRI" badge is on every published race page and offered to the organizer as a snippet on the review page. `/go/embed`, `/go/share` and `/go/badge` count a visit before sending it on, so the embed's link, the badge and a shared post can be told apart in the traffic report. Two mailto templates that linked a path that did not exist link the organizer app.
- **Published, then verified.** Before publishing, the organizer confirms that they organize the race and have the right to publish its results; the confirmation goes with the request and is kept with the race. Every publish is then screened (`api/screening.py`): a race with a strong sign of being invented or copied (a field of placeholder names, a third of the field tied to the second, evenly spaced times, a score at or above the human ceiling, a whole field in world-class territory, a date still ahead, a race called a test, OTRI's own example files, results identical to a race already on OTRI, or a hold an admin gave it before) is held, not public, with the reasons in plain words on the organizer's review page. A clean race is public at once and is marked verified on its own after 24 hours (`OTRI_AUTO_VERIFY_HOURS`) unless an admin acts first. Admins get an email for every publish and decide under Admin → Reviews: verify, hold, or reject with a note the organizer receives. Nothing is approved and there is no certificate: verified means an admin saw nothing wrong. `docs/product/open-scoring-tool.md`, "Published, then verified".
- **A fifth outside review: four findings fixed.** *A course with every other elevation missing cost the square of its length to measure*: the list of known elevations was rebuilt for every point without one, 4.5 s for 32,000 points on the public calculator; it is built once (0.07 s, and 100,000 points in a quarter of a second). *A sign-in with the old password that was already past its password check survived a reset*: the password was checked against one read of the account and the token stamped with a later read of the session version, so a reset landing in the sixth of a second bcrypt takes was carried along by the very sign-in it was meant to end. The token now gets the version that was read together with the hash; a two-factor challenge is only written if that version still stands, with the account row locked, and the second step stamps its token the same way. *A stranger could register your address and put their second factor on it*: registration signs in at once, the address unconfirmed, and with two-factor on top the real owner never got the account back, because a reset rightly leaves the second factor alone. Two-factor now needs a confirmed address, and a reset that is the first proof anybody ever gave of the mailbox takes over the account without whatever second factor was on it. *Two uploads at once could both squeeze under the account's limit of stored results*: the limit is checked inside the transaction that writes, with the account locked. The registration finding (an address that has an account is told so) stays as decided.
- **A small course file could keep a core busy for seconds, anonymously.** The GPX reader took every track segment at any depth and walked the whole subtree of each, so with segments nested inside each other (which no program writes and the schema forbids) a point was read once per segment around it: 400 KB holding two points took two seconds, the cost grew with the square of the file, and the two points came back as 48,000. Every element is now read once, a segment's points are its own children, and segments in the wrong place are refused with a message that says so: 0.02 s. Two checks run before the file is parsed at all, each a millisecond: more than 100,000 points, and far more markup than 100,000 points need (19 MB of empty elements around two points took three seconds). All 31 real course files in the repository read point for point as before.
- **A fourth outside review: three findings, all about what was on its way in when an account's protection changed, fixed.** *A reset link survived a password change*: an owner who changed the password because somebody may have been in the mailbox left that somebody a link that set it again, for up to an hour. Changing the password, signing out everywhere and turning two-factor on or off now end every open reset link and every half-finished sign-in. *A sign-in started with email codes could be finished after the switch to an authenticator*, with the old emailed code and without the authenticator: the switch drops it, and a challenge is only good for the second factor the account has now. *A runner planted in a draft was who a later publication was matched to*: the last fix stopped drafts writing into existing runners, but a draft could still create one, with a year of birth and a nationality of the uploader's choosing, and the first organizer to publish that name without such details got the planted ones on the public profile. An upload is now matched only to runners the public already sees, the organizer's own, and the ones the race's results pointed to before (so a re-upload keeps its runners' ids). Checked on production for the same review: the rate limiter sees real client addresses, not the proxy's; the API and the database listen on loopback only; the allowed origins are the two of otri.run. Registration still says when an address has an account, on purpose: see the API README's known gaps.
- **The calculator says where a score stands: a scale from Beginner to World class.** Under the score: the level's name, a bar of all nine levels with the score marked on it, what the same share of world-record speed is on a flat road marathon, and what time on this course reaches the next level ("Advanced starts at 600: 2:41:52 here, 38:38 faster"). "All levels" opens the table. The names are a reading aid; what they stand for is exact, because a score is a share of world-best speed and the ceiling curve runs through the marathon world best: 700 is a 3:03 marathon, 600 is 3:40, 500 is 4:33. No claim about how many runners are in a level: OTRI has no such data. The FAQ explains it; `tests/unit/test_score_levels.py` keeps the scale's two constants and the times said in words equal to the model's.
- **A media and brand page (`#media`, linked from the footer) with the logo as files.** On the site the wordmark is live text in the visitor's system font, so there was no logo anyone could download. `public/brand/` now holds the logo, the compact logo and the mark, each in colour, for dark backgrounds, white and one colour, as SVG (letters drawn as outlines from Inter and JetBrains Mono, both open licence, so the files need no font) and transparent PNG; a profile picture; a "Scored with OTRI" badge for race websites with its HTML; and the whole kit as a zip with its terms. The page shows which file goes on which background, clear space and smallest sizes, what not to do (shown, not only told), the colours (click to copy), the typefaces, how to write the name, and a description free to quote. It says throughout that OTRI approves no races: "scored with OTRI", never "OTRI certified". Also the icons the site was missing: a PNG favicon, an Apple touch icon, a web manifest with maskable icons. `scripts/build_brand_kit.py` and `scripts/build_brand_png.mjs` rebuild everything.
- **No fast hash touches a password or a credential any more** (two code-scanning alerts). A passphrase longer than bcrypt's 72 bytes was shortened with SHA-256 on its way into bcrypt: sound, but a bare digest of a password is a value other sites' leaks may hold as well, so it is PBKDF2 with a salt of OTRI's own now; no password had been set on production since long passphrases were allowed, so no stored hash changes. One-time tokens (confirmation, reset, sign-in challenge) are kept as PBKDF2 digests too; production had no open link. Signing out no longer stores a digest of the session token at all: it stores the token's id (`jti`), which opens nothing.
- **The admin's Server page says whether the watchdog and the nightly backup are alive.** Both report by email or not at all, so one that never ran looked the same as one with nothing to report: the nightly database dump failed for want of an executable bit on its script (cron calls it directly; git had seven of the ten deploy scripts as plain files, now all executable) and nothing anywhere said so. The Services card now shows the watchdog's timer, its last check and how that ended, and the newest dump with its age and size, red once it is older than 26 hours or empty. `GET /admin/server` carries both as `watchdog` and `backups`.
- **Two more outside security reviews: sixteen findings checked, thirteen fixed, two were already, one is a decision.** *A 1.5 KB workbook cost 14 seconds of a core* (minutes at the full grid) on the public scorer: a sheet that claims 16,384 columns and a million rows was believed, every row padded to that width and every gap filled with empty rows. The claim is dropped, no row is read past 200 columns, and empty rows count towards the row limit: 0.2 s. *The deploy user's sudo rules were root in three commands*: they allowed writing the API's systemd unit, reloading and restarting. The unit is now installed by root (`scripts/deploy/10-install-service.sh`); the deploy only restarts, and says when the unit needs installing again. **On the server, as root, once: `./scripts/deploy/09-harden-sudo.sh` again, to install the narrower rules.** *Sign-in codes were kept in the email log*, in the subject, readable by admins and in the account export, while the database stored only their hash: the log now keeps the subject without the code, and a migration removes the old ones. *Removing an address from `OTRI_ADMIN_EMAILS` removed nothing*: the list is now what counts, on every request; the flag on the account follows it. *The newsletter export wrote names and organizations as typed*: formula cells are made text, as in the scores export. *Turning two-factor on left every other session signed in* for up to thirty days: it signs them out, as turning it off did, and hands the device that did it a fresh session. *Signing out cleared the cookie and left the token valid*: the token is revoked (its digest, until it expires); tokens carry a unique id, so two sign-ins in one second are two tokens. *A draft wrote into runners the public sees*: an upload to an unpublished race, by an unconfirmed account even, could give a known runner a year of birth and a nationality. A draft now only matches; publishing the race completes the runner. *Runners outlived their results*: replacing a race's results, deleting the race or closing the account left the runner rows, so one race re-uploaded with new names grew the table outside every quota. They go with their last result; a migration removes those left behind. *A shared course's budget counted the track and not the file beside it*, where a 4 KB file name was kept whole: both count, the name is cut to 200 characters. *The certificate check connected to whatever the Host header named*: only names that resolve to public addresses, at the address that was checked. *`login_failures` kept a row for every address ever typed*: rows go once their window and lock are over. A reset link and a recovery code are now spent under a row lock, so two requests at the same instant cannot both use one. Already fixed by the audit before: the link in a report (only a page of this site is kept, and React never ran `javascript:` links), and the lockout that survived a password reset. Left as it is, on purpose: registering with an address that has an account says so. Hiding it means not signing a new organizer in until the address is confirmed, and building a race straight away is worth more here; an address tries ten registrations an hour. `tests/unit/test_security_review_3.py` tries each one, and fails on the code before.
- **A security audit of the whole repository: eight findings and four smaller ones, fixed.** (1) *The password asked for again was checked without any limit*: signing out everywhere, deleting the account, turning two-factor off and new recovery codes each run a bcrypt comparison, a sixth of a second of a core, and each is a guess at the password by whoever holds the session. Now limited per account and per address, and ten wrong passwords pause them for the account. (2) *Wrong second-factor codes were counted per challenge only*, and every sign-in hands out a new challenge: whoever had the password could guess at the code for ever. They are now counted per account across challenges; ten pause the second step for an hour, from every address, and the owner gets an email saying somebody knows the password. A password reset does not clear that count. (3) *Fetching terrain tiles could hold the API's worker threads for minutes*: the manifest lock was held across every download of a course (up to 270 s), and the requests behind it waited with their thread in hand; the public calculator reached it. Downloads now run outside the lock, three at a time across workers, 60 s a tile and 90 s a course at most; a request that finds the slots taken measures without terrain instead of queueing. New tiles have an allowance: 6 a day per visitor, 40 per account, 30 an hour for everyone, so a script walking the globe cannot push real courses' tiles out of the disk budget either. Eight concurrent requests for eight new tiles: 27.6 s before, 3.2 s now. (4) *Random ids were 32 bits and a taken one was a server error*: with 100,000 runners stored, a results file of 20,000 new names failed more often than not, all of it, and the next try as likely. A taken id is now drawn again inside the same transaction, and new runner ids are 64 bits. Existing ids stay as they are. (5) *An unconfirmed address could store millions of rows*: it now holds 3 events, 6 distances an event and 20,000 results in all until the address is confirmed (an account 3,000,000); uploads are also limited per hour, and one address makes at most 10 accounts an hour and 30 a day. (6) *The link in a report was the sender's choice*, shown to admins on the dashboard and in the report email: only a page of this site is kept, and the dashboard checks again. Reports are all stored; the emails about them stop at 12 an hour. (7) *Anybody's inbox could be filled with reset and confirmation mail*: three account emails an hour per recipient, whoever asks, with the same answer either way. (8) *Ten wrong passwords locked the owner out*, from anywhere: they now lock out the address they came from; fifty from anywhere lock the account, and a password reset lifts it. Also: sign-in challenges are stored as digests like the other tokens; recovery and email codes are hashed with a key from the environment (a copied database alone no longer yields 40-bit recovery codes; codes issued before keep working); rate limits are sliding windows, so an allowance cannot be spent twice across a minute boundary; a chunked upload, whose size cannot be checked in advance, is refused. Storing results no longer runs on the event loop. The test suite no longer calls the email provider when a key is set machine-wide. `tests/unit/test_security_audit.py` tries each attack, and fails on the code before.
- **Five findings of an outside security review, all confirmed and fixed.** (1) *The two-factor attempt limit never counted*: a wrong code was counted in the same database transaction as the check, and the error it raised rolled the count back, so eight wrong codes left the counter at zero and a six-digit code could be guessed at for the challenge's whole ten minutes. The attempt is now committed before the code is looked at; the sixth guess is refused even when it is right. (2) *A password-reset link got past the second factor*: the reset signed the account in. With two-factor on, a reset now changes the password and signs nobody in; the owner signs in as usual, code included, and the sign-in page says so. (3) *A reset did not sign anyone out*: it now ends every session of the account (the session counter the password change already used), drops half-finished sign-ins and kills any other open reset link. (4) *A session alone could replace the authenticator*: starting the authenticator or the email-code setup now needs the password, like turning two-factor off already did; the account page asks for it first. (5) *A throwaway account could pile up data and keep the course measurement busy*: creating events and races and uploading a course or results are limited per account and per address (the old limit was keyed by URL, so every race was a fresh allowance), an unconfirmed address may hold 3 events, an account 500, an event 40 distances; admins are exempt. `tests/unit/test_security_account.py` tries each attack.
- **The API reference (`/docs`) no longer runs unpinned third-party script on the API's origin.** FastAPI's generated page loads whatever a CDN serves today for `swagger-ui-dist@5` (and ReDoc `@next`), as script on `api.otri.run`, which is where organizers' and admins' session cookies count: one hijacked package would act with the session of whoever opened the page. `/docs` is now served by the API itself with Swagger UI at one exact version, each file pinned by its SHA-384 (Subresource Integrity, so the browser refuses any other bytes), under a Content-Security-Policy that allows that CDN, one hashed start-up script and requests to the API only; ReDoc is off; `/openapi.json` stays public. Every other answer carries `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'`. nginx stops announcing its version (`server_tokens off`; re-run `03-configure-nginx.sh` once for that). The page was checked in Chrome: 68 operations drawn, they open, and the policy blocks nothing it needs. Checked from outside at the same time: a foreign origin is given no CORS permission, anonymous writes and admin routes answer 401/403, plain http redirects, TLS 1.0/1.1 are refused.
- **A security review of the API, with fixes.** Sound already: every SQL value is a bound parameter (the two statements assembled at run time are built from a fixed list of column names and a count of placeholders, and the runner search escapes `%` and `_`); passwords are bcrypt; sessions are signed JWTs with the algorithm pinned and a per-account revocation counter, in an HttpOnly SameSite cookie that only counts on state-changing requests together with the `X-OTRI-Client` header (no cross-site forgery); every write checks the owner; XML is parsed with defusedxml and DTDs are refused; share ids and file names cannot leave their folder; websites and source links can only be `http(s)`; rate limits are per real client address (the proxy appends it, a forged header does not help) with account lockout. **Fixed:** (1) *Drafts were readable by anyone*: `GET /events` listed every organizer's unpublished events, and `GET /events/{id}` and `GET /races/{id}` answered for private ones (names, dates, finisher counts; never results or course files). The public list now holds only events with something published or listed, and a draft answers 404 to everyone but its owner and admins. (2) *A long passphrase was a server error*: bcrypt reads 72 bytes and its current version refuses more, while the site allows 128 characters (25 Thai characters are 75 bytes); longer passwords are now hashed with SHA-256 first, shorter ones as before, and hashes made while older bcrypt truncated still verify. (3) *Sign-in timing told accounts apart*: an unknown address answered without the password check a known one costs; it now does the same work. (4) *Email-confirmation and password-reset tokens were stored as sent*; they are stored as SHA-256 digests, so a copy of the database opens no account (links already sent still work, and a stored digest is never accepted as a token). (5) *A spreadsheet zip bomb*: an `.xlsx` is checked for what it claims to unpack to (200 MB, 2,000 entries) before it is opened. `tests/unit/test_security.py` tries each; `tests/unit/test_api_security.py` covers the draft rule.

- **Terrain tiles are fetched when a course needs them** (`course/dem_fetch.py`, `OTRI_DEM_AUTOFETCH=1`, which the deploy script now writes). - **API attack surface.** GPX parsing goes through defusedxml (entity bombs and external entities are a 422, not a hang or a file read); results files are capped at 50,000 rows and non-spreadsheet zips are a clean 422; bodies over 20 MB are refused before being read; every response carries nosniff, X-Frame-Options DENY and a referrer policy, and anything personal is `Cache-Control: no-store`; `/gpx/analyze` is rate-limited (60/min per IP); GPX parsing, results validation and scoring run off the event loop; runner search escapes LIKE wildcards; every text input has a length limit; upload temp files only ever get a plain extension; the events list makes one query for race counts instead of one per event. nginx config gains HSTS, the same headers and a per-IP request budget (re-run `03-configure-nginx.sh`). `tests/unit/test_api_security.py` checks every route's auth requirement from the app's own route table, ownership, forged tokens, injection strings, traversal, parser bombs, mass assignment, enumeration and headers.
- **Calculator slider** spans each course's own range, from the human-best time (score 1000) to a slow finish (score 200), instead of a fixed 10 minutes to 24 hours; typed times still go anywhere up to 200 hours.
- **Organizer sessions moved into an HttpOnly cookie.** The web app no longer stores the token in the browser's storage, so a cross-site-scripting bug could not exfiltrate a session; the cookie is SameSite=Lax and Secure behind HTTPS, state-changing requests need the app's `X-OTRI-Client` header (CSRF guard), and `POST /auth/logout` ends the session on that device. Bearer tokens keep working for API clients, scripts and earlier sign-ins.
- **Production hardening.** Sessions carry a per-account version and are revoked by password change, turning 2FA off, "Sign out everywhere" and account deletion; rate limits moved to the database (shared by all workers, survive restarts) with a per-account lockout after ten wrong passwords; numbered one-shot schema migrations (`api/migrations.py`, `scripts/migrate.py`) on top of the idempotent baseline; every email send logged with its Resend id (`/admin/emails`, counts on the admin overview); `/health` for the watchdog and uptime checks; optional Sentry via `SENTRY_DSN`; self-service data export and account deletion on the account page; shared-course eviction serialized so concurrent uploads cannot exceed the budget. Droplet scripts: backups now integrity-checked, include shared courses and `.env`, keep 30 days and copy off-site with rclone when configured; a restore script with a scratch-database test mode; a systemd watchdog timer (auto-restart, disk and service alerts by email); a sudoers file that limits the deploy user to the commands the scripts need; the deploy script keeps optional `.env` settings across redeploys. The organizer profile no longer asks for a phone number.
- **Calculator:** a "What is a GPX, and where do I get one?" note under the course step, with the full guide in `docs/WHAT-IS-A-GPX.md`.

- **The production scoring model is now named `OTRI model 0.1.0`** everywhere users see it (`docs/methodology/OTRI-MODEL-0.1.0.md`). It consolidates the development builds V0.1–V0.8 into one specification; no formula, constant or stored score changes. The API keeps returning the build id `0.8.0-course-standard-power` as `scoring_version` so every stored score replays exactly; the site maps that id to the public name and shows any older build id as a superseded build. Public model names change (`0.2.0`, …) only when scores can change.
- **Organizer accounts: sign-in security and profile.** Remember me (30-day session, ticked by default) or a 12-hour session; two-factor authentication with an authenticator app (TOTP) or emailed codes, with ten one-time recovery codes; a password policy at sign-up and reset (10–128 characters, no common passwords, not built from the email) with a strength meter; an account page for profile details (name, organization, website, country, phone, bio), password change and two-factor settings; organization and website appear on the organizer's public race pages. Sign-up now requires accepting `TERMS.md` and `PRIVACY.md` and records when; an optional, revocable newsletter consent is stored with its timestamp and exported for admins as `/admin/newsletter.csv`.

- **New default scoring model `0.8.0-course-standard-power`** (`docs/methodology/v0.8/OTRI-POWER-CURVE.md`). One power law for the whole scale, `score = 1000 × f^0.85` with `f` the runner's fraction of the human-ceiling rate; the last V0.1 demo anchors (349, 544) retire. V0.6/V0.7 were already `1000 × f^0.692` above 544, a concave shape that flattered the middle of the field (53% of the ceiling scored 643). The top holds — world bests 957–1000, the 100-mile mountain winner 958 — and every score below drops, more so further down: a runner at 53% goes 643 → 581, a last finisher at 22% goes 371 → 274. 0.85 is a stated judgement, not a fit and not a reference to any third-party index. V0.7 and earlier remain selectable and reproducible.
- **`course-measurement-v3`: geodesics and smoothing in C/numpy.** Same formulas, windows and thresholds as v2; `pyproj.Geod` replaces the pure-Python `geographiclib` solves and the smoother runs in numpy. Agreement with v2 on three real courses, end to end: distance within 1.4e-8 m, gain/loss within 5.5e-9 m, every profile point within 2.3e-8 m, flags and scores identical (the reference 100-miler 966, CM6 707, Phuket 554) — but profile hashes differ, hence the version bump. Measured live after deploy: the reference 100-miler end to end 7.9 s → 4.4 s cold (2.3 s cached; 61.6 s before the sampler and cache work), score unchanged at 987 on the DEM; measure step 0.51 → 0.12 s locally. The first request each worker serves after a restart also pays the one-time tile checksum (~1.5 s). Stored v2 measurements replay unchanged. `pyproj` is now a runtime dependency.
- **DEM measurement ~8-35x faster, and cached.** Profiling on the production droplet (1 CPU, 1 GB) showed `RasterProvider.sample()` re-hashing every tile on each call and issuing one windowed GDAL read per bilinear neighbour per 10 m grid point. Tiles are now checksummed and opened once per process, and sampling is vectorised in numpy (one bounding-window read per tile per course); the result is bit-identical to the scalar implementation, which is kept as a test reference. The API also caches each measurement on disk (`data/cache/measurements/`, keyed by file hash + terrain manifest, bounded), so the prototype's repeated analyses of the same course re-score without re-measuring. Measured live: Reference 100-miler, 171 km 61.6 s → 7.9 s cold / 2.3 s cached; Phuket 14.6 km 9.8 s → 0.28 s / 0.03 s; scores and profile hashes unchanged.
- **New default scoring model `0.7.0-course-standard-dem-gated`** and **`course-measurement-v2`** (`docs/methodology/v0.7/OTRI-DEM-GATED-MEASUREMENT.md`). The curve is V0.6's, unchanged; what changes is what a score is allowed to claim. Under V0.6 the same route recorded by two devices measured up to 25% apart and the score said nothing. Measurement v2 records the track's median point spacing and marks anything sparser than 30 m `needs_review` (such tracks chord the switchbacks and measure the course short; at or under 30 m the error stays within ~2–3% on four real courses). When a pinned Copernicus GLO-30 manifest is configured (`scripts/deploy/06-install-dem.sh`), elevation comes from it instead of the uploaded file; a course the tiles do not cover falls back to uploaded elevations *announced*, never silently. Every V0.7 score now carries a confidence label — `High` only with DEM elevation on a dense track, otherwise `Low` with the reason in `quality_flags` — shown in the prototype. V0.6 and all earlier models, and stored v1 measurements, remain selectable and reproducible. Confidence gates on route reproducibility only (sparse or disconnected geometry, or implausible *uploaded* elevation when the upload is the source); the scoring-domain grade flag still marks a measurement for review but no longer blocks `High`, and uploaded-point noise is not held against a DEM-sourced measurement. DEM-sourced production scores differ a few % from the uploaded-elevation pins in the V0.5/V0.6 notes on alpine courses (the reference 100-miler 987 vs 966) — documented, not re-tuned.
- **New scoring model `0.6.0-course-standard-smoothed-upper`** (`docs/methodology/v0.6/OTRI-SMOOTHED-UPPER-CURVE.md`). V0.5 unchanged except that V0.1's 692 demo anchor is dropped, so a single power law runs from the 544 anchor to the world-best 1000 anchor. The 544→692 segment was ~45% more elastic than its neighbours, an artefact of fitting through three demo finishers, and it amplified every uniform course-level correction in the 550–700 band — V0.5 lifted the reference race's 2:40 finisher by 63 points but its winner by only 50. V0.6 halves that mid-pack lift, leaves every score at or below 544 identical to V0.5, and keeps the mountain-ultra winner at 966. Because the curve is universal, road runners who sat in the same inflated band come down too (a 1:45 half: 700 → 667). V0.5 and all earlier models remain selectable and reproducible.
- **New scoring model `0.5.0-course-standard-terrain-adjusted`** (`docs/methodology/v0.5/OTRI-TERRAIN-ADJUSTED-DEMAND.md`). Keeps V0.4's score curve and instead adjusts **course demand** for what the Minetti gradient-cost integral does not price: that polynomial was measured on a treadmill, so it understates sustained steep mountain terrain and ignores altitude entirely. Both are now measured from the course GPX — the share of distance at or above 20% grade, and distance-weighted elevation above 1500 m — and applied as a terrain factor on demand. Road courses have a factor of exactly 1.000 and are untouched; an elite mountain 100-mile performance moves from 889 to 970. Two consequences to note: the steepness term cannot distinguish technical footing from plain steepness (GPX does not contain it), so it lifts all steep courses together, and V0.1's demo/test anchors therefore shift under this model (the reference race's 2:20:30 moves from 692 to 737). `STEEP_COEFFICIENT` is calibrated to a single real performance — see the methodology note's limitations before treating it as measured. V0.4 and all earlier models remain selectable and reproducible.
- **New scoring model `0.4.0-course-standard-endurance-referenced`** (`docs/methodology/v0.4/OTRI-ENDURANCE-REFERENCED-CURVE.md`). A score now measures what fraction of the best humanly-possible performance on a course of that demand the runner achieved, so race length no longer moves the score: the same-calibre run scores the same at 5 demand-km and at 700, and 1000 means world-best at every course size. This fixes two defects at once — V0.3's Riegel `b=1.06` correction was derived from sub-4-hour road racing and under-corrected badly beyond it (a real 100-mile mountain-race winner scored 783), while V0.1's 1000-anchor was an acknowledged extrapolation that saturated the short end (a 15:06 5 km also scored 1000). V0.1's three real score anchors (349/544/692) are unchanged; only the invented 1000-anchor and the course-size scaling are replaced. The short segment of the new reference curve independently reproduces Riegel's published `b=1.06` to three decimals over the range his data covered. `0.3.0-course-standard-duration-scaled` and all earlier models remain selectable and reproducible.
