# Changelog

All notable OTRI releases and material methodology changes will be documented here.

## Unreleased

- **The course map reads as a trail map, and runner profiles can be shared.** The route and the elevation profile are coloured by steepness, the way race profiles are drawn (greens to reds for climbs, blues for descents, in classes that end at the 20 % the score's terrain factor counts as steep ground; `src/lib/steepness.js`), with a switch to turn it off. Under the profile: the whole course as one bar, the share of the distance that climbs, is flat and descends, the share of steep ground, the lowest and highest point, and what the colours mean. On the map: contour lines with heights, worked out in the browser from the elevation tiles the hillshade already used (`maplibre-contour`, no new service), footpaths and tracks picked out as dashed brown lines, summits with their height, stronger relief, and distance markers that do not fight the coloured line. A GPX that holds a route and no track is now drawn too. **A runner's profile has “Share as an image” and “Copy the link”**: the index and the best three races as a picture in the three formats (`drawRunnerCard`), with a post written for it that starts in the first person (“My OTRI index”) and switches to the runner's name with “Someone else”. Checked in Chrome.
- **A pass over the small things, on both sites** (`src/lib/comfort.js`). **A link opens the next page at its top, and Back returns to where you were**: with hash routes the browser did neither, so a footer link showed the next page's footer, the organizer's steps opened scrolled down, and Back dropped you at the top of a list. **Drag and drop works**: the pages said “drop a file here” and nothing took it, so the browser opened the dropped file and left the site; now the calculator, both organizer upload steps and Score my race take dropped files (on Score my race both files at once, anywhere on the form, told apart by their ending), a file of the wrong kind is refused in words, and a file dropped beside a field no longer navigates away. **The outcome comes into view**: after “Validate and score” in the organizer app the report or the scores scroll into view and take the focus, as the result on Score my race does; errors too. Also: a quiet back-to-top button after a screen and a half, “/” puts the cursor in the page's search box, a show/hide switch on every password field, the cursor starts in the first field of the sign-in, sign-up, new event and new race forms (not on touch screens), and smooth scrolling is skipped for visitors who asked their system for less motion. The FAQ says plainly that a score above 1000 is not a bug, and why it happens. Checked in Chrome.
- **“Which races can OTRI score?” is answered where people bring a race.** One table (`src/components/WhatWeScore.jsx`), stating the model's limits as the code has them: trail and mountain races and road races, yes; vertical races, courses steeper than 45% and efforts under about 1.5 flat-km, scored and provisional (Low confidence, with the reason); races with no fixed course (24-hour, backyard, relays, a stage race as a whole), no. Under it, what a score does not see: technical ground, mud, snow, heat, darkness. It is the second question of the FAQ (found by searching road, vertical, backyard, relay …) and a fold-out line on the calculator and on Score my race. Two FAQ answers about what an organizer needs and which files are accepted were brought up to date with the tolerant results upload.
- **Organizer app: four steps, and less typing.** The steps are event, race and course, results, review: four, numbered the same on every page (“Step 1 of 4 · Event”). The account is not a step, since whoever reads a step number has one; the welcome page says “Four steps” and the account pages “Organizer account”. The location field suggests some 145 towns and areas where trail races are held (`src/lib/placeNames.js`), and choosing one fills in the country while it is still empty (Chamonix: France); the race name of a distance suggests the usual listings (“50K”, “100 mile”, “Vertical”, …). Both stay free text. The note about the scoring model under the new-race form is gone, and the sign-up page no longer says the email must be confirmed before adding a race (it is only needed to publish). The home page's opening adds “no black box: every number explains itself” and “made for the local race as much as the famous one”. Checked in Chrome with a throwaway account on the test database.
- **Course files: messages a person can act on, and routes are read.** A review of what the API answers for files it cannot use found the results side sound and the course side not: messages carried the server's temp-file path and the parser's vocabulary (“C:\…\tmpvry1fn89.gpx: document root must be gpx”, “'utf-8' codec can't decode byte 0x8b”). Every message now says what the file is and what to do: a KML, TCX, FIT, PDF, zip/kmz or .gz is named as such with how to get a GPX; an empty, damaged or cut-short file, one with only waypoints, a single point, several tracks (their names are listed) or no elevation says so in words; a GPX under 100 m (a recording of the start area) is refused instead of being scored at 1. **A GPX that holds a route (`rtept`, what route planners export) and no track was refused and is now measured**, and a course saved as UTF-16 or with a byte-order mark is read. On the results side a missing column is said once, not once more per row, and a file named .xlsx that is not a workbook says what to do. In the browser, a file over 20 MB or an empty one is refused before anything is sent, naming the file (the server's refusal at that size is a dropped connection, not a message); a rate limit, a restart and a timeout have their own sentences; a missing file reads “The course file (GPX) is missing”, not a JSON list; and the visitor is no longer asked whether the API is running. `tests/unit/test_upload_messages.py` pins the messages and that none carries a path.
- **Results upload: bring the export you already have.** Only a finish time and a name are needed now; everything else is read where the file has it (`ingestion/normalize.py`). Absorbed: semicolon- and tab-separated files, UTF-16 and Windows-1252 text, title lines above the header, a unit in a header; one name column instead of two (“WALMSLEY Jim”, “Walmsley, Jim”, “Jim Walmsley”); gender inside a category (SEH, V1F, M40-44, W35); nationality as FR, France, Frankreich or the Olympic GER (a country table generated from the package the website uses); times as 12h34m56s, 12h34'56'', 1d 02:03:04, a spreadsheet time or duration cell; DNF or Abandon written in the time column; any birth-date layout (only the year is kept); no rank column, or one that is a category ranking (positions are then worked out from the times, equal times sharing one; a “Category rank” column is never taken for the overall one); rows with nothing in them count as DNF. Headers are matched in English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch and Thai. **What stops an upload is only what a score cannot be made without** (a finisher with no readable time, a runner with no name, several distances in one file); a position given twice, a time out of order with its position or a repeated bib were errors and are warnings, and a message repeated on many rows is listed fifteen times and then counted. Every answer carries `columns` and `ignored_columns`, and both upload pages show “How your file was read”. `.xls` is refused with what to do. Tested against the shapes of an ITRA / UTMB sheet, a RaceResult list, a LiveTrail download, a Thai spreadsheet and a bare finisher list, written from their layouts: no real export was at hand, so the first real files may still show a header to add.
- **The races and runners searches know the well-known names.** Both boxes already offered the best matches among the races and runners on OTRI. They now also offer well-known races (`src/lib/raceNames.js`) and some 60 well-known trail runners (`src/lib/runnerNames.js`, names only) that match and are not here, marked "not on OTRI yet": choosing one puts the name in the box, and the page says why it is empty (OTRI shows what organizers publish, it keeps no list of every race or runner) and what to do instead: work out a time's worth in the calculator, or score the whole race. OTRI records no searches, so "most searched" is a hand-picked list. The forgiving matching (accents, punctuation, word order) is shared in `src/lib/suggest.js` with the calculator's race search. Checked in Chrome.
- **The example race moved from a Bangkok suburb to trail country.** The synthetic 24 km loop sat at 13.62 N 100.48 E, in the flat southern outskirts of Bangkok, with invented hills. With terrain tiles fetched on demand that was more than a joke: a server would fetch the real, flat terrain there and measure the invented hills away, changing every score in the example. The loop now lies over the forested hills west of Hang Dong, south-west of Chiang Mai, and its elevations are read from the Copernicus tile for that ground (`scripts/generate_example_race.py`), so it measures the same with terrain data (24.0 km, +927 m) and from the file alone (+944 m): under half a percent apart in scored demand. It is still an invented loop, not a marked trail, and says so in the file. A drawn loop straight over Doi Suthep came out at +2,800 m with half its distance steeper than 20 %; this spot gives a trail-like +930 m with 14 % steep ground. Winner 1:56:16 for 792, as before.
- **Calculator: a race search that knows the well-known races.** "Pick a race" (no longer "Search a verified race") ignores accents, punctuation, case and word order ("geants tor" finds Tor des Geants) and ranks names that start with what was typed first. Well-known races from `src/lib/raceNames.js` that match and have no course on OTRI are shown under the results as "no course here yet"; choosing one, or searching for something nobody has, explains what to do: look for the GPX on the organizer's website (with a web search for it one click away), upload it, or write to the organizer (a prepared email saying that listing a race is free). Checked in Chrome.
- **Home page: one door for runners, one for organizers.** A runner arriving on the home page met an organizer's pitch and a card with labels like "CALCULATE · any course · any time". The hero now says what OTRI is in one sentence and offers two cards: "I run: What is my time worth?" (open the calculator, find my results) and "I organise a race: Score my whole race" (score my race, see an example), with the API as a text link below. The dark card explains the score itself ("Course + time = score": one number for a finish time on any course, 1000 is world-record level, never dependent on who else raced), and its rows are sentences that name their audience.
- **Calculator: the minutes and seconds fields take two digits again, and the course comes first.** Typing "20" into minutes gave 02: after the second digit the focus moves on to seconds, which blurs the minutes field, and its blur handler still held the half-typed "2" and committed it over the 20. The draft now lives in a ref that is emptied before the focus moves, the typed digit is read from the keystroke and not from the text in the box, and an emptied field keeps its value. Checked in Chrome by clicking into the fields and typing (20, 59, 75 capped at 59, a single digit then clicking away, backspace). Before a course is chosen, "Start with the course" (search a race or upload a GPX) is now the first thing on the page, above "Know your score before you race", so a visitor starts at once; with a course chosen the score stays on top.
- **Calculator: the line under the score says what it means.** "103% of the world-best rate for this course: faster than any human has run ground like this" is now "103% of world-record speed for a course like this", followed by the time a world-record-level run would take on this course (which scores 1000) and, above 1000, that the target is faster than that. The bar and the slider say "1000 = world-record level".
- **A deploy that changes the deploy script now applies it at once.** `02-deploy-app.sh` pulls the code itself, so bash went on running the copy it had started with: a new `.env` setting or systemd option only took effect on the deploy after (this is why tiles on demand were not switched on by the deploy that brought them). The script now starts over, once, when the pull changed it.
- **Terrain tiles are fetched when a course needs them** (`course/dem_fetch.py`, `OTRI_DEM_AUTOFETCH=1`, which the deploy script now writes). Elevation comes from Copernicus GLO-30 tiles on OTRI's own disk; until now they were installed by hand, region by region, and a course anywhere else scored at Low confidence. The whole dataset is some 26,000 tiles and several hundred GB, so instead the API downloads the 1°×1° tiles under a course the first time a course there is measured, from the same public bucket, checks each is a readable north-up EPSG:4326 GeoTIFF, checksums it and adds it to the manifest: from then on it is pinned like an installed tile, measurement still runs on local files only, and stored races keep their own measurement snapshot. Fetched tiles are kept within `OTRI_DEM_BUDGET_MB` (default 8192; a tile is 5 to 45 MB, so a few hundred tiles): beyond it the ones unused for longest are deleted; tiles installed with `06-install-dem.sh` never are, and that script keeps the fetched entries when it rewrites the manifest. Guard rails: at most 6 tiles per course, one download at a time across workers, open sea is remembered for 30 days, and any failure leaves the course measured as before, from its own elevations at Low confidence. The provider now verifies and opens a tile the first time a course needs it, not every tile whenever the manifest changes, accepts tiles of different pixel widths (GLO-30 narrows above 50° latitude), and the measurement cache is keyed to the tiles under the course, so a tile fetched elsewhere does not discard other courses' measurements. The admin overview shows a tile count, the first codes and the budget used. **Production needs `02-deploy-app.sh` re-run once**: the service's sandbox must be allowed to write to `/opt/otri/dem`.
- **The score is no longer capped at 1000** (part of build `0.10.0`, OEP-003). 1000 is what the human-ceiling rate for a course scores, a reference point and not a limit: a time faster than the ceiling curve now scores above 1000 (the 1500 m world record 1020, the half marathon 1005) instead of being flattened to it. No score of 1000 or less changes. A target time can be asked for a score above 1000, and the calculator's slider reaches 1100, with chips for 900 and 1000.
- **Calculator: a target time that is easy to set, and a slider that starts where it says.** The slider did not start at the time that scores 500: the code that works that time out only ran for build ids containing "-power", which no current build does, so it fell back to a pace guess. It now uses the course's ceiling time whatever the build is called. The time is three fields (hours, minutes, seconds) in the large display itself: type digits and it moves on, arrow keys step it, plus −5/−1/+1/+5 minute buttons and "what time scores 300 … 800" chips that set the time for that score. The separate HH:MM:SS text box is gone. "How the math works" no longer lists raw quality flags, the build id or the methodology file path; what a runner should know from the flags (unverified elevation, a sparse track, a provisional vertical score, ground beyond the model's range) is said in words under the score.
- **Vertical races are scored** ([OEP-003](docs/governance/oep/OEP-003-scoring-vertical-races.md); new build id `0.10.0-course-standard-vertical`, still OTRI model 0.1.0). An uphill-only course (more than half its distance at 20 % grade or steeper) uses a steep coefficient of its own, 0.1169 instead of 0.5951: the mountain coefficient mostly prices descending and broken ground, which a vertical race does not have, and it over-scored such courses by about 40 %. It is calibrated on one performance (a winning 36:59 on a 3.76 km / +1,016 m vertical kilometre scores 970), so every vertical score is `Low` confidence with the reason `vertical_calibration_provisional`. **Every course scored before is scored identically**: of 28 reference outputs compared entry by entry, the three that differ had no score. A vertical race without its course file is still listed with finish times only. Migration `0005_vertical_build` moves stored races to the new build id.
- **Calculator: two ways to share, side by side.** Next to "Share this score" (the link, as before) there is now "Image and post text": it opens a full-width panel under the hero with the target as an image (post, square or story), a post written for it that carries the share link once one exists, and download, copy and share buttons. It was a small text link inside the share box before. A hashtag is made from the race name only when it makes a usable one.
- **Copy: publishing first, not privacy first.** The home page, Score my race, the API page and the share panel no longer lead with "nothing kept" and "nothing is stored": results are facts a race wants to show, so the wording now says free, no account, no approval, and publish with one click. What happens to uploaded files is still stated, once, on the API page and in the privacy notice; the behaviour is unchanged.
- **Less typing.** The country field is a type-ahead box (`src/components/CountrySelect.jsx`): "thai", "TH", "THA", "UK", "Holland" or "south korea" all find their country, with flags, arrow keys and Enter, the visitor's own country offered first, and the same alpha-3 code stored as before. The races and runners searches offer the best six matches to jump straight to a race or a runner (`src/components/SearchSuggest.jsx`); the lists below still filter as you type. Race-name fields (Score my race, create event, publish a scored race) suggest some 200 well-known trail races from `src/lib/raceNames.js`; the field stays free text.
- **Score my race shows more of the race.** The result now has the course on the map with its elevation profile, country and gender as columns, striped rows (also on public leaderboards), and the page scrolls to the result when it arrives. `#score?example=1` scores the example race on load, and the home page links to it as "See an example result". Podium images scale their rows to fill the frame, so a top 3 is as full as a top 10.
- **A UX pass in a real browser, desktop and phone.** The home page leads with scoring a race (a new first section, the hero card and the closing band point to the same two entries: score a race, calculate a target time), with a shorter opening and the open-source line on its own. On a phone the Score my race form follows the headline and the example instead of sitting under the reassurances. Stale "Course Standard" wording, a stray section number on the races page and the wide units menu in the organizer header are gone.
- **An invitation to contribute.** The home page's opening now ends on the GitHub link and "you can help improve both", which leads to a Contribute block in the Method section (`#contribute`, also in the footer): challenge the scoring model (through an OEP), improve course measurement, report what looks wrong, or write code, each with its link into the repository.
- **Share images and post text.** After scoring a race (Score my race) and on every published race page, "Share the podium" makes a picture of the top 3, 5 or 10, overall or women or men, in three formats (post 4:5, square, story 9:16), with a post written to go with it: the podium with times and scores, a sentence on what the score means, the link when the race has a page, and hashtags. In the calculator a runner gets the same for a target time: the score large, the course, the time, the share of the human ceiling, and a caption with the share link. The text is a suggestion to edit; the image is drawn in the browser on a canvas (`prototype/shareImage.js`, `prototype/SharePanel.jsx`), downloaded or handed to the phone's share sheet, and nothing is uploaded, stored or posted by OTRI.
- **An example race on Score my race.** "Use the example race" fills in a synthetic 24 km course and a results file of 100 made-up finishers (John Doe, Max Mustermann and their counterparts in other countries) with four DNF and two DNS; "Show rows" previews the file's layout, and both files can be downloaded. The field is derived from the model itself (`scripts/generate_example_race.py`, fixed seed): the winner scores 790, the median 448, nobody 1000. The course is dense enough to measure and sits where no terrain data is installed, so it always scores the same, at Low confidence with the reason shown. A test scores the committed files through `POST /score`.
- **The calendar is gone; every race stays.** The Calendar view of the races page, "Add to calendar" and the `GET /calendar.ics` feed are removed: OTRI is not a race calendar. The races page lists every published and organizer-listed race as before. The organizer's course step no longer warns when the official figures differ from the measured course by more than 5% (the comparison is still shown next to each figure).
- **One scoring model in the code.** The eleven older scoring versions (the development builds `0.1.0` to `0.8.0`, the two legacy logarithmic curves and the field-relative baseline) were removed; what remains is OTRI model 0.1.0, build id `0.9.0-course-standard-domain-gated`, in a `scoring/course_standard.py` a third of its former size. Its output was compared before and after on every course fixture, official figures and the raw curve: identical to the last digit. `GET /scoring/models` lists one model and a retired `scoring_version` is refused (422) on create and edit. Migration `0004_single_scoring_model` moves races stored under a retired build to the model: builds `0.8.0` and later gave the same scores, earlier ones did not, so such a race's scores change once. Removed with them: the raw-points demand pipeline (`compute_course_demand`), `scripts/calibration_report.py`, `scripts/test_otri_phuket_200.py`, and the tests of the retired builds (the suite goes from 444 tests to about 350, with the curve and ceiling properties consolidated in `tests/unit/test_scoring_curve.py`).
- **Email confirmation no longer blocks signing in.** `POST /auth/register` signs the new organizer in (a session like `/auth/login`, plus `message`), so they build their race straight away; `POST /auth/login` no longer answers 403 for an unconfirmed address. What needs the confirmed address is making a race public: `POST /races/{id}/publish` and `POST /races/{id}/listing` answer 403 "confirm your email address first" until then. `email_verified` is on the session answers and `/auth/me`; an unconfirmed address is never an admin, even if it is in `OTRI_ADMIN_EMAILS` or flagged in the database; a completed password reset confirms the address. The organizer app shows a bar with "Send it again" until the link has been opened, and picks the confirmation up when the tab regains focus.
- **From a scored race to a race page in one click.** After scoring, Score my race invites the organizer to **Publish this race**: the course and the results are handed to the organizer app through the browser (IndexedDB, `prototype/publishHandoff.js`; nothing is sent by that step, and it is deleted once used or after a day), so nothing is uploaded twice. The new `#/publish` screen in the organizer app shows what is waiting, offers account creation or sign-in (sign-in now lands there, and the account screens say the race is waiting), asks only for the event name, the race date and the distance name, then creates the event and race, attaches the course and submits the results step by step, and ends on the review page where Publish remains a separate press. The organizer welcome page points the other way too: score first without an account, publish afterwards.
- **OTRI-compiled listings, requests for scores, claims and race suggestions are retired.** OTRI keeps no catalogue of races and does not decide who owns one ([`docs/product/open-scoring-tool.md`](docs/product/open-scoring-tool.md)). Removed: `POST /admin/listings` and the CSV import, the bulk course upload, the permission note on a course nobody owned, `POST /races/{id}/score-requests` ("I'd like scores", "Ask your organizer"), the claim forms with `GET /events/matches`, `POST /events/{id}/claim` and `POST /admin/events/{id}/assign`, and "Suggest a race" with `POST /admin/reports/{id}/create-listing`; report kinds `claim` and `suggestion` are no longer accepted. Race summaries no longer carry `is_claimed`, `request_count`, `official_url` or `course_permission`. An organizer can still list their own race before it has results, and the calendar and its feed now show only those. Nothing is dropped from the database; a listed race that nobody owns is no longer public, and an admin deletes such leftovers from the events list.
- **Score my race needs the course file.** `POST /score` does not score from an official distance and climb: a score rests on where the climbing is.
- **OTRI is an open scoring tool, not an approval body** ([`docs/product/open-scoring-tool.md`](docs/product/open-scoring-tool.md)). **Score my race** (`#score`, `POST /score`): a course file (GPX, required) and a results file in, the validated and scored result list out, as JSON or CSV, with the course's confidence, quality flags and any reason it is not scored. No account, and nothing is kept: both files are deleted before the answer is sent and no race, result or runner is written. It is the same validation and scoring as a published race (`_scored_rows` serves both). The three tool endpoints (`/score`, `/gpx/analyze`, `/scoring/models`) now answer any origin without credentials, so a race website or a timing system can call them from the browser; every other route stays on the allow-list. New **API and embed** page (`#api`) with examples, limits and what is kept, and an **embeddable calculator** (`prototype/embed/`, a fourth build entry) for an iframe on a race's own site: `?race=`, `?gpx=`, `&t=`, and it reports its height to the host page. The home page, README and handbook say what OTRI is and is not: no approving of organizers or races, no promise of a complete race list, no official rankings before the model is validated.
- **Listings import at calendar scale.** Admin → Events & races → Import CSV takes a file of up to 19 MB (it was 2 MB and 5,000 rows) with an "only races from" day, 1 January by default, so a calendar file with years of history imports this season only (`POST /admin/listings/import?since=`; the reply counts the rows left out as `before_since`). Everything already on OTRI is read once and the new events and races are written as one transaction: a few thousand races take about a second, where the old importer read every event again for each one. At most 10,000 races per import, and the list of skipped rows is cut at 100. The same name and date is judged on a normalised name, so "Doi-Trail 2027" does not become a second event next to "Doi Trail". To carry that many listings, API responses are gzip-compressed (the race list is one response the browser filters), `GET /admin/events` reads the races in one query instead of one per event, the admin events list has a search box and shows 40 at a time, and the public calendar draws the nearest months (about 120 events) with "Show later months".
- **Creating an event that is already on the calendar offers the listing instead.** An organizer who created their event from scratch while it was listed unclaimed put the race on the calendar twice, with the runners' requests for scores left on the old entry. The create-event form now looks the name and date up as they are typed (`GET /events/matches`: a loose name match within three days, see `api/event_match.py`) and shows what is already there: an unclaimed listing, with "This is my race, claim it", or the organizer's own event, with a link to it. A claim from the form (`POST /events/{id}/claim`) is a claim report from a verified account that names the event, so Admin → Reports has "Hand over the event" on it. Nothing changes hands before an admin has checked it, and the organizer can still create a new event when the match is wrong.

- **Race calendar.** The races page has a Calendar view (`#races?view=calendar`, filters kept in the address so a filtered calendar is a shareable link): upcoming events by month with a countdown, one row per event with its distances, courses on OTRI highlighted for a target time before race day. Every event and every upcoming race page has "Add to calendar" (`.ics` and Google); `GET /calendar.ics` is a subscribable feed (`webcal://`, optional `?country=`). Runners can **suggest a race** from the calendar (facts only); it reaches the admins as a report carrying the facts, and Admin → Reports → Create listing puts it on the calendar in one step after a check against the race's website. The view switch and the suggestion form are there even when no race is public yet.
- **Demo races are not public by default.** `scripts/seed_demo_data.py` (run on every deploy) creates the synthetic demo races unpublished and leaves the publish state of existing ones alone; `--publish` shows them all (local and staging sites), `--unpublish` hides them all. Admins still see them under Admin → Events & races and can publish one by hand. The home page's races section appears only when there is a scored race to show, and says "Demonstration races" only when that is all there is.
- **Course permission on unclaimed listings is optional.** A course file can be attached to a race nobody owns without a permission note (single and bulk upload). A note is still recorded when given, and only then does the public page say the course is shown with permission; otherwise it says "course file". The policy in `DATA_POLICY.md` is unchanged in substance: the uploader answers for the right to publish the file.
- **Uploaded course files are sanitized before they are stored.** Every path that keeps a GPX (a race's course, single or bulk upload, and a shared calculator course) now stores and serves positions and elevations only: no author, device, timestamps, heart rate or other extensions, waypoints or notes (`course/sanitize.py`). Coordinates round-trip exactly, so the stored file measures bit-identically to the upload (tested on every fixture). What the file said about its origin (creator, author, copyright holder, licence, links) moves to the race's private record next to `raw_sha256`, and is not returned by the public measurement endpoint; the permission rules for course files are unchanged. `scripts/sanitize_stored_gpx.py` (dry run by default, `--apply` to rewrite) cleans files stored before this change.
- **Bulk course upload for admins.** The Listings box on the admin page takes many GPX files at once, matches each to a race without a course by its file name (a guess the admin can change), and attaches them one at a time with one shared permission note or a note per file. Unclaimed races are still refused without a recorded permission.
- **Race listings** ([`docs/product/race-listings.md`](docs/product/race-listings.md)). A race can be shown on the races page before anyone has uploaded results: admins add unowned listings from race facts (one at a time or by CSV), and organizers can list their own upcoming race. A listing page shows the facts, the course when OTRI may show it, an "I'd like scores" button with a public count (one per visitor, no personal data stored), a message the runner can send to the organizer, and "I organize this race", which files a claim in the admin reports queue; an admin then assigns the event to the organizer's account. Results stay behind publishing. A course file on an unclaimed listing is refused without a recorded licence or permission, in line with the rule against copying GPX files from the web. - **Vertical races, and a new build id `0.9.0-course-standard-domain-gated`** ([OEP-002](docs/governance/oep/OEP-002-domain-gating-and-vertical-races.md); still OTRI model 0.1.0, every existing score unchanged). - **New default scoring model `0.8.0-course-standard-power`** (`docs/methodology/v0.8/OTRI-POWER-CURVE.md`). One power law for the whole scale, `score = 1000 × f^0.85` with `f` the runner's fraction of the human-ceiling rate; the last V0.1 demo anchors (349, 544) retire. V0.6/V0.7 were already `1000 × f^0.692` above 544, a concave shape that flattered the middle of the field (53% of the ceiling scored 643). The top holds — world bests 957–1000, the 100-mile mountain winner 958 — and every score below drops, more so further down: a runner at 53% goes 643 → 581, a last finisher at 22% goes 371 → 274. 0.85 is a stated judgement, not a fit and not a reference to any third-party index. V0.7 and earlier remain selectable and reproducible.
- **`course-measurement-v3`: geodesics and smoothing in C/numpy.** Same formulas, windows and thresholds as v2; `pyproj.Geod` replaces the pure-Python `geographiclib` solves and the smoother runs in numpy. Agreement with v2 on three real courses, end to end: distance within 1.4e-8 m, gain/loss within 5.5e-9 m, every profile point within 2.3e-8 m, flags and scores identical (the reference 100-miler 966, CM6 707, Phuket 554) — but profile hashes differ, hence the version bump. Measured live after deploy: the reference 100-miler end to end 7.9 s → 4.4 s cold (2.3 s cached; 61.6 s before the sampler and cache work), score unchanged at 987 on the DEM; measure step 0.51 → 0.12 s locally. The first request each worker serves after a restart also pays the one-time tile checksum (~1.5 s). Stored v2 measurements replay unchanged. `pyproj` is now a runtime dependency.
- **DEM measurement ~8-35x faster, and cached.** Profiling on the production droplet (1 CPU, 1 GB) showed `RasterProvider.sample()` re-hashing every tile on each call and issuing one windowed GDAL read per bilinear neighbour per 10 m grid point. Tiles are now checksummed and opened once per process, and sampling is vectorised in numpy (one bounding-window read per tile per course); the result is bit-identical to the scalar implementation, which is kept as a test reference. The API also caches each measurement on disk (`data/cache/measurements/`, keyed by file hash + terrain manifest, bounded), so the prototype's repeated analyses of the same course re-score without re-measuring. Measured live: Reference 100-miler, 171 km 61.6 s → 7.9 s cold / 2.3 s cached; Phuket 14.6 km 9.8 s → 0.28 s / 0.03 s; scores and profile hashes unchanged.
- **New default scoring model `0.7.0-course-standard-dem-gated`** and **`course-measurement-v2`** (`docs/methodology/v0.7/OTRI-DEM-GATED-MEASUREMENT.md`). The curve is V0.6's, unchanged; what changes is what a score is allowed to claim. Under V0.6 the same route recorded by two devices measured up to 25% apart and the score said nothing. Measurement v2 records the track's median point spacing and marks anything sparser than 30 m `needs_review` (such tracks chord the switchbacks and measure the course short; at or under 30 m the error stays within ~2–3% on four real courses). When a pinned Copernicus GLO-30 manifest is configured (`scripts/deploy/06-install-dem.sh`), elevation comes from it instead of the uploaded file; a course the tiles do not cover falls back to uploaded elevations *announced*, never silently. Every V0.7 score now carries a confidence label — `High` only with DEM elevation on a dense track, otherwise `Low` with the reason in `quality_flags` — shown in the prototype. V0.6 and all earlier models, and stored v1 measurements, remain selectable and reproducible. Confidence gates on route reproducibility only (sparse or disconnected geometry, or implausible *uploaded* elevation when the upload is the source); the scoring-domain grade flag still marks a measurement for review but no longer blocks `High`, and uploaded-point noise is not held against a DEM-sourced measurement. DEM-sourced production scores differ a few % from the uploaded-elevation pins in the V0.5/V0.6 notes on alpine courses (the reference 100-miler 987 vs 966) — documented, not re-tuned.
- **New scoring model `0.6.0-course-standard-smoothed-upper`** (`docs/methodology/v0.6/OTRI-SMOOTHED-UPPER-CURVE.md`). V0.5 unchanged except that V0.1's 692 demo anchor is dropped, so a single power law runs from the 544 anchor to the world-best 1000 anchor. The 544→692 segment was ~45% more elastic than its neighbours, an artefact of fitting through three demo finishers, and it amplified every uniform course-level correction in the 550–700 band — V0.5 lifted the reference race's 2:40 finisher by 63 points but its winner by only 50. V0.6 halves that mid-pack lift, leaves every score at or below 544 identical to V0.5, and keeps the mountain-ultra winner at 966. Because the curve is universal, road runners who sat in the same inflated band come down too (a 1:45 half: 700 → 667). V0.5 and all earlier models remain selectable and reproducible.
- **New scoring model `0.5.0-course-standard-terrain-adjusted`** (`docs/methodology/v0.5/OTRI-TERRAIN-ADJUSTED-DEMAND.md`). Keeps V0.4's score curve and instead adjusts **course demand** for what the Minetti gradient-cost integral does not price: that polynomial was measured on a treadmill, so it understates sustained steep mountain terrain and ignores altitude entirely. Both are now measured from the course GPX — the share of distance at or above 20% grade, and distance-weighted elevation above 1500 m — and applied as a terrain factor on demand. Road courses have a factor of exactly 1.000 and are untouched; an elite mountain 100-mile performance moves from 889 to 970. Two consequences to note: the steepness term cannot distinguish technical footing from plain steepness (GPX does not contain it), so it lifts all steep courses together, and V0.1's demo/test anchors therefore shift under this model (the reference race's 2:20:30 moves from 692 to 737). `STEEP_COEFFICIENT` is calibrated to a single real performance — see the methodology note's limitations before treating it as measured. V0.4 and all earlier models remain selectable and reproducible.
- **New scoring model `0.4.0-course-standard-endurance-referenced`** (`docs/methodology/v0.4/OTRI-ENDURANCE-REFERENCED-CURVE.md`). A score now measures what fraction of the best humanly-possible performance on a course of that demand the runner achieved, so race length no longer moves the score: the same-calibre run scores the same at 5 demand-km and at 700, and 1000 means world-best at every course size. This fixes two defects at once — V0.3's Riegel `b=1.06` correction was derived from sub-4-hour road racing and under-corrected badly beyond it (a real 100-mile mountain-race winner scored 783), while V0.1's 1000-anchor was an acknowledged extrapolation that saturated the short end (a 15:06 5 km also scored 1000). V0.1's three real score anchors (349/544/692) are unchanged; only the invented 1000-anchor and the course-size scaling are replaced. The short segment of the new reference curve independently reproduces Riegel's published `b=1.06` to three decimals over the range his data covered. `0.3.0-course-standard-duration-scaled` and all earlier models remain selectable and reproducible.
