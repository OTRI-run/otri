# Changelog

All notable OTRI releases and material methodology changes will be documented here.

## Unreleased

- **`course-measurement-v3`: geodesics and smoothing in C/numpy.** Same formulas, windows and thresholds as v2; `pyproj.Geod` replaces the pure-Python `geographiclib` solves and the smoother runs in numpy. Agreement with v2 on three real courses, end to end: distance within 1.4e-8 m, gain/loss within 5.5e-9 m, every profile point within 2.3e-8 m, flags and scores identical (UTMB 966, CM6 707, Phuket 554) — but profile hashes differ, hence the version bump. Measure step on the droplet's core: ~4.7 s → ~1 s for UTMB (0.51 → 0.12 s locally). Stored v2 measurements replay unchanged. `pyproj` is now a runtime dependency.
- **DEM measurement ~8-35x faster, and cached.** Profiling on the production droplet (1 CPU, 1 GB) showed `RasterProvider.sample()` re-hashing every tile on each call and issuing one windowed GDAL read per bilinear neighbour per 10 m grid point. Tiles are now checksummed and opened once per process, and sampling is vectorised in numpy (one bounding-window read per tile per course); the result is bit-identical to the scalar implementation, which is kept as a test reference. The API also caches each measurement on disk (`data/cache/measurements/`, keyed by file hash + terrain manifest, bounded), so the prototype's repeated analyses of the same course re-score without re-measuring. Measured live: UTMB 171 km 61.6 s → 7.9 s cold / 2.3 s cached; Phuket 14.6 km 9.8 s → 0.28 s / 0.03 s; scores and profile hashes unchanged.
- **New default scoring model `0.7.0-course-standard-dem-gated`** and **`course-measurement-v2`** (`docs/methodology/v0.7/OTRI-DEM-GATED-MEASUREMENT.md`). The curve is V0.6's, unchanged; what changes is what a score is allowed to claim. Under V0.6 the same route recorded by two devices measured up to 25% apart and the score said nothing. Measurement v2 records the track's median point spacing and marks anything sparser than 30 m `needs_review` (such tracks chord the switchbacks and measure the course short; at or under 30 m the error stays within ~2–3% on four real courses). When a pinned Copernicus GLO-30 manifest is configured (`scripts/deploy/06-install-dem.sh`), elevation comes from it instead of the uploaded file; a course the tiles do not cover falls back to uploaded elevations *announced*, never silently. Every V0.7 score now carries a confidence label — `High` only with DEM elevation on a dense track, otherwise `Low` with the reason in `quality_flags` — shown in the prototype. V0.6 and all earlier models, and stored v1 measurements, remain selectable and reproducible. Confidence gates on route reproducibility only (sparse or disconnected geometry, or implausible *uploaded* elevation when the upload is the source); the scoring-domain grade flag still marks a measurement for review but no longer blocks `High`, and uploaded-point noise is not held against a DEM-sourced measurement. DEM-sourced production scores differ a few % from the uploaded-elevation pins in the V0.5/V0.6 notes on alpine courses (UTMB 987 vs 966) — documented, not re-tuned.
- **New scoring model `0.6.0-course-standard-smoothed-upper`** (`docs/methodology/v0.6/OTRI-SMOOTHED-UPPER-CURVE.md`). V0.5 unchanged except that V0.1's 692 demo anchor is dropped, so a single power law runs from the 544 anchor to the world-best 1000 anchor. The 544→692 segment was ~45% more elastic than its neighbours, an artefact of fitting through three demo finishers, and it amplified every uniform course-level correction in the 550–700 band — V0.5 lifted the reference race's 2:40 finisher by 63 points but its winner by only 50. V0.6 halves that mid-pack lift, leaves every score at or below 544 identical to V0.5, and keeps the mountain-ultra winner at 966. Because the curve is universal, road runners who sat in the same inflated band come down too (a 1:45 half: 700 → 667). V0.5 and all earlier models remain selectable and reproducible.
- **New scoring model `0.5.0-course-standard-terrain-adjusted`** (`docs/methodology/v0.5/OTRI-TERRAIN-ADJUSTED-DEMAND.md`). Keeps V0.4's score curve and instead adjusts **course demand** for what the Minetti gradient-cost integral does not price: that polynomial was measured on a treadmill, so it understates sustained steep mountain terrain and ignores altitude entirely. Both are now measured from the course GPX — the share of distance at or above 20% grade, and distance-weighted elevation above 1500 m — and applied as a terrain factor on demand. Road courses have a factor of exactly 1.000 and are untouched; an elite mountain 100-mile performance moves from 889 to 970. Two consequences to note: the steepness term cannot distinguish technical footing from plain steepness (GPX does not contain it), so it lifts all steep courses together, and V0.1's demo/test anchors therefore shift under this model (the reference race's 2:20:30 moves from 692 to 737). `STEEP_COEFFICIENT` is calibrated to a single real performance — see the methodology note's limitations before treating it as measured. V0.4 and all earlier models remain selectable and reproducible.
- **New scoring model `0.4.0-course-standard-endurance-referenced`** (`docs/methodology/v0.4/OTRI-ENDURANCE-REFERENCED-CURVE.md`). A score now measures what fraction of the best humanly-possible performance on a course of that demand the runner achieved, so race length no longer moves the score: the same-calibre run scores the same at 5 demand-km and at 700, and 1000 means world-best at every course size. This fixes two defects at once — V0.3's Riegel `b=1.06` correction was derived from sub-4-hour road racing and under-corrected badly beyond it (a real 100-mile mountain-race winner scored 783), while V0.1's 1000-anchor was an acknowledged extrapolation that saturated the short end (a 15:06 5 km also scored 1000). V0.1's three real score anchors (349/544/692) are unchanged; only the invented 1000-anchor and the course-size scaling are replaced. The short segment of the new reference curve independently reproduces Riegel's published `b=1.06` to three decimals over the range his data covered. `0.3.0-course-standard-duration-scaled` and all earlier models remain selectable and reproducible.
- Establish professional repository structure.
- Add MIT licensing metadata.
- Formalize documentation, data, test, script, and source-code areas.
- Organizer accounts now require email verification before signing in.
- Organizers manage events with multiple race distances (full create/edit/delete), each with an optional attached GPX file, instead of a single flat race record.
- Scoring is now pluggable: a new default "Course Standard" model (`docs/methodology/v0.1/OTRI-SCORING-SYSTEM-V0-CODE-SPEC.md` — Minetti gradient-cost course demand + a logarithmic 0-1000 scale clipped at two published reference points) scores runners purely on course and finish time, with no dependency on competitors; the previous field-relative model is kept selectable per race distance.
- Recalibrated the Course Standard model's default scale anchors (`1.1.0-course-standard`: 500 points at 3.5 demand-km/h, 1000 points at 10.5 demand-km/h) after real-world testing showed the spec's own literal anchors (15.0/22.5 demand-km/h) implied absurdly fast absolute finish times (~70 minutes for 500 points) regardless of course length, clustering realistic multi-hour trail finishes at 0. The spec-literal curve remains selectable as `1.0.0-course-standard` for spec-fidelity comparisons.
